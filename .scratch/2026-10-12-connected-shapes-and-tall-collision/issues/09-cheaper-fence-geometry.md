# Cheaper fence geometry, so more of them fit in a Chunk

Status: done
Blocked by: 07

Spec decision 10's outcome. A Chunk of 1024 reference fences meshed in 5.2 ms
against 1.5 ms for 1024 stairs, so a connected Shape is charged `cap_units`
(7 stairs for the fence) and 146 fit in a Chunk. That protects the mesh budget
and caps what a player can build: a fenced field hits it.

## What to decide

The cost is quads, and a fully armed fence emits several times a stair's: each
arm is two rails, and each rail is its own box with side, top, bottom and end
quads, and the post's side faces are split around where the rails join them.
Two levers, which are not exclusive:

- **The reference fence.** One flat slat per arm instead of two rails, or
  rails that stop short of the post's faces, emits fewer quads. This changes
  how the fence looks and the numbers in `shape::examples`, `meshing.rs` and
  the gallery, and nothing in the engine. Mods own their own Shapes, so this
  helps the reference only.
- **The engine.** `extract_quads` merges only within one plane of one Shape.
  Rails of neighbouring fences are collinear and coplanar and could merge into
  one long quad per rail across a fence line, which is also what pass 2's
  per-Voxel emission forbids ("nothing merges across Voxels"). That is a real
  change to pass 2 and to the hole-free property, so it wants its own spec.

## Acceptance

- `cap_units` for the reference fence drops to 4 or fewer, by cheapening its
  geometry. The arms no longer look like two rails; that is decided (see
  Comments), and the gallery capture is re-taken.
- `terrain_fences_at_cap`, at the new cap, adds no more over bare terrain than
  `terrain_stairs_at_cap` does, which is the cap's rule (the constant's doc
  comment states it: the Chunk at the cap costs at most 50% of
  `mesh_chunk/straddling` on top of the terrain).
- The weight is derived, not hand-set: change the geometry and `cap_units`
  follows.
- Merging rails across Voxels is not part of this ticket; it is
  `.scratch/2026-10-12-merged-rails-across-voxels/`.

## Triage Notes

**Category:** enhancement. **State:** `ready-for-agent`.

**Decided with the maintainer:**

- Both levers are pursued, as separate work. This ticket takes the reference
  fence's geometry; the engine lever gets its own spec and tickets, because it
  cannot satisfy this ticket's acceptance (below).
- The reference fence's look changes. It is a reference a Mod replaces, so cost
  wins over keeping two rails; the original "without the arms looking
  different" line is dropped, as one bar per arm cannot look like two rails.

**Findings that shaped the split:**

- `cap_units` is the worst case of one Voxel: `quads(voxel, all arms)` over a
  stair's quads, rounded up. Merging rails across Voxels does not shrink that
  Voxel, so the engine lever cannot lower `cap_units` and cannot meet the "4 or
  fewer" target. It could only lower real mesh time, and only if the cap were
  redefined; that is the merge spec's question.
- Where the 7 comes from, by hand from the reference fence (to be confirmed by
  the agent, not trusted): a fully armed fence is about 70 quads, against about
  10 for a stair. The post's four side faces are 7 quads each, cut around the
  two rail joints (28), its top and bottom 2, and each of four arms is two
  rails of 5 visible faces (40). One bar per arm gives about 38: each side face
  is 4 quads around a single joint (16), plus 2, plus four arms of 5 (20). That
  is 4 stairs.
- Rails "stopping short of the post" is not a lever. `extract_quads` works on
  one rasterised grid, so a rail overlapping into the post emits the same
  quads, and one that stops short leaves a gap and adds a sixth face per rail.
- The ticket's "within +50% of `mesh_chunk/straddling`" is the cap's rule
  abbreviated. The rule is the added cost over terrain (see Acceptance). Ticket
  07 records 1.5 ms for 146 fences on a machine at load ~100 without saying how
  that compares to the rule, so measure the old cap's number in the same
  interleaved run as the new one.

## Agent Brief

**Summary:** Give the reference fence one bar per arm instead of two rails, so
its cap weight drops from 7 stairs to 4 or fewer.

**Current behavior:**
The reference fence is a post with two rails per arm. Fully armed it emits
roughly seven times a stair's quads, so `ShapeTable::cap_units` charges it 7 and
146 fit in a Chunk. A fenced field reaches that.

**Desired behavior:**
Each arm of the reference fence is one box, the arm's width, set between the
post's top and bottom, and the post and the collision are otherwise unchanged
(collision stays 1.5 Voxels high and per arm; this is a drawing change). Its
`cap_units` is 4 or fewer and follows from the geometry with no hand-set number.
The doc comments that quote 7 and 146 quote the new numbers.

**Key interfaces:**

- The reference fence's `ShapeDef` (`shape::examples`): the arm's `boxes`. Its
  arm `collision` and the post are untouched.
- `ShapeTable::cap_units`: no change in logic. Its test already asserts the
  weight is the minimum that covers the worst case, and must still pass.
- Tests, benches and the dev gallery that state or depend on the arm boxes
  (the shape test that lists each arm's box, the meshing tests that count a
  fence's quads, the `terrain_fences_at_cap` and `flat_fences_at_cap` benches)
  follow the new geometry.

## Done

Done. Each arm is one bar (7/16 to 13/16 up) instead of two rails, and nothing
else about the fence changed; its collision is still 1.5 Voxels high.

**`cap_units` is 4, so 256 fit** (was 7 and 146), derived from the geometry. The
arm tests that stated the rail boxes, the ray test (which had a gap between the
rails to pass through, and now has under and over the bar), and the 16-box bound
test (it needed 12 extra boxes to cross the bound, not 8) follow the new shape.

Interleaved A/B, four rounds, old and new bench binaries alternately, load 2-7,
Apple M5, medians:

| Bench                                      |    old |    new |
| ------------------------------------------ | -----: | -----: |
| `greedy_mesh/terrain`                      | 337 us | 336 us |
| `greedy_mesh/terrain_stairs_at_cap`        | 550 us | 550 us |
| `greedy_mesh/terrain_fences_at_cap`        | 535 us | 547 us |
| `greedy_mesh/flat_fences_at_cap`           | 483 us | 492 us |
| `mesh_chunk/straddling`                    | 400 us | 398 us |

Old is 146 fences, new is 256. A Chunk of fences at the cap adds 211 us over
bare terrain, against 214 us for stairs at theirs, so the cap's rule holds with
no more margin than stairs have. The quad counts I estimated by hand (70 and 38)
were not checked directly; the weight of 4 is what the registered Shape yields.
