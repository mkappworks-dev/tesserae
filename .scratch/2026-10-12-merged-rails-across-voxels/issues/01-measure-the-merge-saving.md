## Agent Brief

**Category:** enhancement
**Summary:** Count, without changing the mesher, how many pass-2 quads a
cross-Voxel merge would remove, and how often the light allows it.

**Current behavior:**
Pass 2 emits every shaped Voxel's quads on its own. The savings from merging
collinear faces across Voxels are estimated by hand at about 20% for a fence
line and nothing for an isolated fence, and are unmeasured.

**Desired behavior:**
A throwaway counting helper (a test or bench-local function, not part of the
mesher) takes a `ChunkMesh`'s pass-2 output, or the equivalent per-Voxel quads,
and reports how many quads would remain after merging under the spec's
decision 1. The helper is not merged into the mesher.

**Key interfaces:**

- The merge rule of decision 1 of the spec: same face and plane, abutting along
  a full Voxel-boundary edge, same Material, render layer and `Light`.
- The existing bench scenes: a fence line, `terrain_fences_at_cap`,
  `flat_fences_at_cap`, a floor of slabs, `terrain_stairs_at_cap`, `all_stairs`.

## Comments

Measured with a throwaway probe (an integration test that reads each mesh's
pass-2 quads back as rectangles and runs a greedy merge under decision 1; not
kept, not in the mesher). Apple M5, release, flat full-sky light unless stated.
Fences are the cheaper one-bar fence from ticket 09. "Pass 2" is the quads the
mesher emitted for shaped Voxels; terrain scenes subtract pass 1's count, which
is exact for fences and off by the few cube faces a stair seals for stairs.

"Lost to light" is the share of the merges that geometry allows which a
differing `Light` blocks, from merging once with light in the key and once
without.

**What this says that the hand estimate did not.** The fence estimate held
(about 20%, and none for an isolated fence), and it is fragile: next to a light
source block light changes every Voxel, so a line merges not at all. But the
spec's decision 2 (every shaped quad) is where the value is: slab and stair
tops are coplanar across Voxels and merge to a fifth or less, up to 99.6% for a
flat floor, and they survive light much better because sky light is flat over
open ground. Merging arms alone would have been the smaller part of the win.

**Cost of the buffering.** The prototype's extract-and-merge (read the quads
back, hash by key, sort, merge) takes 354 us on terrain with 256 fences, against
a 575 us mesh, and 216 us on terrain with 1024 stairs, against 494 us. That is
an upper bound: it re-reads vertices and allocates, where pass 2 holds the
quads as integer rectangles already, but it is not small, and the real number
is the implementation's to measure.

**Proposed go/no-go: go, on the generic scope.** Threshold, for the maintainer to
accept or change: pass 2 with merging must add at most +25% to
`terrain_stairs_at_cap` and `all_stairs` over today's, and not move pass 1, the
light solvers or `greedy_mesh/terrain`, `/solid`, `/checkerboard`; in exchange
terrain with 1024 stairs emits 80% fewer quads and a stair or slab floor 95-99%
fewer. If the real cost cannot stay under +25%, the answer is no-go and ticket
02 is `wontfix`. Merging does not change `cap_units`, because the cap bounds mesh
time and per-Voxel emission still dominates it; review question 3 (a cap on
measured quads) is the question this result makes more interesting, and stays
out of scope.

Ticket 02 stays `needs-triage` until the maintainer accepts the go and the
threshold.
